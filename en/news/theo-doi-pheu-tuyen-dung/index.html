Track each recruitment step to identify the real bottleneck

For businesses11/22/2026

ShareFacebookLinkedIn

Distinguish enquiries, appointments and actual arrivals instead of judging a campaign by a single total.

In this article
  1. Agree on definitions before counting
  2. Use an example to check how ratios are interpreted
  3. Make one focused change and review it

1. Agree on definitions before counting

A submitted phone number is not the same as a confirmed appointment. Define the stages clearly: enquiry, contacted, suitable for discussion, appointment booked, arrived and received. Use an internal tracking ID for each person to avoid counting someone twice across channels.

2. Use an example to check how ratios are interpreted

In a hypothetical example, 40 appointments lead to 28 arrivals: an appointment-attendance rate of 70%. This is meaningful only when the period and recording rules match. Do not divide this week’s arrivals by another week’s appointments, or treat an illustrative ratio as a results guarantee.

Hypothetical example of an appointment attendance rate
MetricHow to read it
Confirmed appointments40 appointments in the same period.
Recorded as present28 people attended as scheduled.
Attendance rate28 ÷ 40 = 70%.
Not yet recorded as present12 people have no attendance record. This does not tell you whether they declined, rescheduled or left the job.
  • Record the source, appointment date and latest status.
  • Distinguish a missed appointment, a rescheduling request and a withdrawal.
  • Record reasons applicants actually provide rather than assumptions.

3. Make one focused change and review it

If many booked applicants cannot find the entrance, clearer directions may help more than increasing sourcing spend. If many withdraw after speaking with an adviser, compare the advert with the actual job details. Record when a change was introduced and compare groups with similar conditions before drawing conclusions.
